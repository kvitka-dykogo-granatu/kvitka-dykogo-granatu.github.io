    X-Raying a Research Institute

    Master thesis, AVIZ team at Inria Saclay, April to September 2026. Supervised by Petra Isenberg and Jean-Daniel Fekete. Best Poster Award, IEEE VIS 2026.

    The managers of a research centre wanted to see their 76 teams over time: members, publications, collaborations. The records sat in four systems that did not agree on names or identifiers. I merged them into one warehouse and built the tool on top.

    • Matching rules in SQL for teams, people and institutions. An author counts as matched only when team membership in the same year confirms the name: 20,432 confirmed links out of 35,269 candidates. Under-credit, never mis-credit.
    • Every build runs 22 assertions, checksums and a planted violation. A failed build never reaches the tool.
    • A local language model (Qwen 3.6) against an NMF topic model for sorting 12,027 publications into 14 themes, judged on signals neither model had seen. The language model's themes matched the declared themes for 61.7% of pairs; NMF reached 47.4%.

    More detail
    • One parser per source. Each download goes into a dated file that is never changed afterwards. Python only decodes; everything else is SQL inside DuckDB.
    • One command rebuilds the warehouse in a median of 154 seconds. A build replaces the live one only after every check passes. The safe swap caught two failed builds.
    • The interface: a circle-packed overview, a team view, a comparison of selected teams and a collaboration circle, in one HTML file with D3. Deployed behind Inria's login.
    • Requirements study with the centre's project committee: a survey (6 answers) and a 25-minute card-sorting workshop (8 people in 3 groups), turned into 7 requirements.
    • Evaluation: two one-hour sessions with centre managers, plus 5 researchers on the ICE-T questionnaire (means 5.26 to 5.67 out of 7). One manager found a real bug; my audit found two more of the same kind.

    Built with Python, SQL in DuckDB, JavaScript, D3 v7, Qwen 3.6, NMF, uv.

    Machine learning

    Fine-grained bird species classification

    Class Kaggle competition, Machine Learning course, CentraleSupélec, January to February 2026. Team of two; I built the whole pipeline.

    20 bird species, about 60 photos each, and some pairs that look the same even to a human. We finished 1st on the private leaderboard with 92% accuracy. 2nd place had 88%.

    • Baselines first. A frozen ResNet-50 scored 48%, fine-tuned about 70%. EVA-02 Large, pretrained on iNaturalist photos of living species, beat ConvNeXt-Base by 4 points in the same pipeline. Everything after it gave 2 more.
    • 5-fold cross-validation on all 1,212 images (96.95% ± 0.43%) replaced a 103-image validation split, because one image moved the score by a full point.
    • A confusion matrix traced 78% of the remaining errors to two look-alike pairs. Small specialist models step in when the main model hesitates between them.

    More detail
    • Bird detection with YOLOv8, and Grounding DINO with text prompts for the 13 test photos YOLO missed. Boxes computed once and cached.
    • A 304-million-parameter model on 1,212 images: a tiny learning rate for the backbone, a faster one for the new head, warm-up, label smoothing, Mixup and CutMix, gradient clipping, early stopping.
    • Test-time augmentation with 4 views per photo (original, flipped, zoomed, both). Logits averaged.
    • Experiments one change at a time, with the same folds and seed, every run logged. Weight averaging and model soup did not hold up and were dropped. The specialists changed 3 of 400 test predictions: one image on the public score, nothing on the private one.

    Built with PyTorch, timm, YOLOv8, Grounding DINO, Hugging Face Transformers, Albumentations, OpenCV, scikit-learn, Google Colab.

    Machine learning

    Building segmentation on satellite images

    Bachelor thesis, Igor Sikorsky Kyiv Polytechnic Institute, February to June 2024. Grade 95 of 100.

    How far can you get without labels, and how much better are pretrained networks, on the same satellite tiles and the same metrics? Three label-free methods against U-Net, U-Net++ and FPN.

    • 8,366 tiles from the Mapping Challenge dataset with 71,871 building outlines, turned into pixel masks and split 8:1:1.
    • The networks, with ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 encoders and Dice loss, reached about 0.80 IoU. The best label-free method reached 0.37, and that score used the true masks to pick the right cluster, so it is an upper bound.
    • Found later, while reviewing the code: a modified network with dilated convolutions was never switched on, so its small gain was noise. Since then I check that a change is really in the model before I compare numbers.

    More detail
    • K-Means and Fuzzy C-Means with 3 clusters; each cluster became a candidate mask. Morphological active contours sometimes selected everything except the buildings, so each mask was compared with its inverse: 0.23 to 0.37 mean IoU.
    • Training on a Kaggle P100 GPU within 30 free hours a week: 320 × 320 tiles, Adam, learning rate 1e-4, batch 16, 10 to 15 epochs, model chosen on validation IoU.
    • Why IoU and not accuracy: most pixels are background, so a model that predicts no buildings still scores 0.96 accuracy.

    Built with PyTorch, segmentation-models-pytorch, Torchvision, Albumentations, OpenCV, scikit-image, scikit-fuzzy, scikit-learn, Kaggle.
